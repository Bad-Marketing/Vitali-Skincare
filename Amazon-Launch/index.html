Vitali Skincare

Amazon Launch Strategy

Prepared by BAD Marketing  ·  Amazon Department

Strategic Focus & Performance Snapshot

Build a strong Amazon ecosystem that supports customer acquisition, product discovery and long-term customer retention.

Strengthen every stage of the Amazon customer journey - from search and product discovery to education, cross-selling and long-term customer retention. Expand the regenerative skincare ecosystem while creating a more connected customer experience that makes it easier for shoppers to discover, understand and adopt Vitali's products.

Current Performance Snapshot
  • H1 performance was heavily impacted by inventory constraints, with the active portfolio declining from five products to one by July. Current business performance should therefore be viewed within the context of limited product availability.
  • The current Amazon experience doesn't fully reflect the strength of the brand. Customer education, product progression and the broader regenerative skincare experience remain underdeveloped across Amazon.
  • Search demand validates a significant growth opportunity. High search volume across copper peptides and GHK-Cu highlights strong potential to expand non-branded customer acquisition.
  • The 2% GHK-Cu Serum has naturally emerged as the hero product, generating the strongest search visibility and serving as the ideal entry point into the portfolio.
  • Customer feedback has been positive, with no meaningful customer experience concerns identified.
  • Subscribe & Save is well aligned with the category. Vitali's protocol-based positioning and focus on consistent daily use create a strong foundation for recurring purchases.
  • Brand assets remain underutilized. Listings, Premium A+, Brand Story, Store and video assets present a significant opportunity to strengthen customer education, product discovery and long-term retention.
Growth Area 1: Build the Amazon Customer Journey

Build an Amazon experience that introduces shoppers to Vitali's regenerative skincare philosophy while guiding them naturally through education, product discovery and complete skincare routines.

Key Recommendations
  • Refresh Listings to better communicate regenerative science, product differentiation and long-term skincare benefits.
  • Redesign Premium A+ around ingredient education, protocols and expected skincare timelines.
  • Develop a catalog-wide video strategy showcasing product application, ingredient education, routines and regenerative skincare protocols.
  • Rebuild Brand Story to introduce the brand philosophy while guiding customers through the broader catalog.
  • Transform the Brand Store from a product catalog into an educational destination organized around routines, collections and customer concerns.
  • Strengthen cross-selling across Listings, Premium A+, Brand Story and Store to encourage routine adoption and product progression.
Growth Area 2: Expand the Amazon Skincare Catalog

Establish Amazon as a complete destination for Vitali's regenerative skincare portfolio by bringing the broader ecosystem to the platform.

Key Recommendations
  • Launch/reactivate hero products to establish a complete regenerative skincare offering.
  • Position the 2% GHK-Cu Serum as the primary entry point into the ecosystem.
  • Build clear product progression across concentrations, formulations and advanced regenerative treatments.
  • Introduce protocol-based bundles and complementary skincare routines as the portfolio expands.
  • Ensure the Amazon catalog reflects the broader regenerative skincare ecosystem available across the brand.
  • Launch Creator Connections campaigns focused on educating customers, demonstrating routines and building trust in regenerative skincare.
Growth Area 3: Increase Customer Lifetime Value

Encourage long-term customer retention by building consistent skincare routines rather than individual product purchases.

Key Recommendations
  • Position Subscribe & Save as a natural extension of long-term regenerative skincare.
  • Reinforce routine adoption through educational messaging focused on cumulative results.
  • Expand cross-selling between complementary products to encourage complete skincare routines.
  • Increase multi-product adoption through protocol-based bundles.
  • Reinforce long-term skincare habits across Listings, Premium A+, Brand Story and Store.
Creative Direction

Creatives need to work to capture both existing branded audiences as well as reassuring and grasping new customers by reinforcing 'believe the science, not the promise'. Scientific support behind the products needs to be immediately visible throughout assets across the platform.

Many customers on Amazon searching for copper peptide products are left feeling as though they're taking a risk on whether the product is actually going to work. We need to reinforce the trust in Vitali through our assets.

Core messaging: The Blue that Transforms Skin. We should make this the signature across every Amazon asset - creating distinction from others in the category, and utilising it as a visual shortcut for "this is the real, potent one".

Positioning on Amazon
  • Category: Regenerative skincare / copper peptides - not "anti-aging." Hold the line on the brand's language (Regenerative, Restore, Longevity), not the trend-y beauty vocabulary competitors use.
  • The wedge vs. competitors: purity and potency you can trust (99.7% pure GHK-Cu*), the visible blue as proof of activity, real founder credibility (Terry & Debbi came out of retirement over this), and a "smart entry point" story around the 2% serum.
  • Premium, but accessible. The look should feel clinical-luxury and earned — never discount, never hype-burst graphics. "Affordable luxury," not "cheap."
  • Hero-led. The 2% GHK-Cu Serum carries the account (strongest search visibility, the entry point). Creative investment should concentrate there first, then radiate out to the routine.
Target Shopper (Amazon-specific)

Two shoppers hit these listings, and the creative has to serve both:

  • Non-branded searchers — searching copper peptides / GHK-Cu / crepey skin, don't know Vitali. High volume, high skepticism. They need fast differentiation (the blue, purity, founders) and education (why this beats the cheap ones). This is the acquisition audience the strategy doc is chasing.
  • Branded / returning shoppers — know Vitali, want the routine, are Subscribe & Save candidates. They need routine-building and cross-sell.

Demographic note to reconcile: the onboarding doc says 45–70; the creative guide says 35–75 (woman) and 35–60+ (man), ~40% male. Recommended to cast to the mature, real-skin end of that range and deliberately include men.

  • The activated-serum blue is the emotional color — reserve it for the "proof" moments (serum smears, drops, the droplet mark) so it stays special.

Photography
  • Product: clean/white or soft-neutral backgrounds, sharp focus, macro texture — blue serum drops, cream smears, the droplet down the frosted-glass bottle. Texture is the point.
  • Lifestyle: bright, airy, natural light. Real, healthy mature skin — pores allowed, minimal retouching. Diverse, authentic casting including at least one male-led scene.
  • Skin-in-use: application shots (serum on fingertip, pressing into cheekbone) read as ritual + credibility. Pull from the founder/"answered by Debbi" style the brand already uses.
Typography
  • Lato / Roboto for overlays (Light–Regular for body, Bold for headers). Clean, sans-serif, generous spacing.
  • Keep overlay copy short and high-contrast. This is a mature audience on mobile — legibility over cleverness.
Graphic Treatment
  • Minimal, clinical-premium, lots of white space. Blue accent rules, thin dividers, small clean icons (paraben-free, cruelty-free, hypoallergenic — verify each before use).
  • Use the RestoraCell / copper-peptide device as a recurring credibility mark.
  • Avoid: sale-burst starbursts, heavy drop shadows, cluttered "10 benefits" grids, and anything that reads DTC-promo. Premium restraint is the differentiator.
Content & Message Hierarchy

Priority order every asset should respect:

  • 1. The blue = it's actually working (differentiation + proof)
  • 2. What it does for me (benefit territory: crepiness, firmness, texture — see Compliance Flags on wording)
  • 3. Why it's trustworthy (purity %, decades of GHK-Cu research, founders, clean formulation)
  • 4. Why 2% is the smart start (removes the "should I go stronger?" hesitation)
  • 5. The routine + how to use (consistency → cumulative results → S&S)
  • 6. The system / cross-sell (moisturizer, eye & lash, exosome, 3%)
Asset Specific Direction
Main Image
  • Make the frosted-white bottle + silver pump + blue droplet detail read premium and crisp. This is a recognition asset, so keep it consistent across every SKU.
  • Reserve the blue serum smear for secondary images (it doesn't belong on a compliant main image).
Image Stack and Premium A+
  • Hone in on the blue being the proof - differentiator
  • Introduce benefits visually early on (which concern it targets through images)
  • Emphasize ingredient credibility
  • Build out how-to-use routine to show simplicity and highlight consistency-led efficacy
  • Lean into founder/brand trust
  • Cross-sell and promote S&S
Brand Story
  • Lead with the founders and the "why no one was talking about this ingredient?" origin — it's genuinely differentiated and human.
  • Establish the category (regenerative, not anti-aging) and route shoppers into the broader catalog (2% → 3% → exosome → routine).
Storefront
  • Shift from product catalog to educational destination
  • Navigation by concern (Crepey Skin, Fine Lines & Wrinkles, Discoloration, Texture/Scarring, Dry & Sensitive) - maps directly to how shoppers self-diagnose.
  • New-to-brand path: clear "Start Here → 2% GHK-Cu Serum" entry.
  • Product progression and protocol bundles as dedicated sections.
  • Homepage hero: the Blue + regenerative promise.
What to Avoid
  • Discount/hype visual language (bursts, "SALE" energy) — it kills the premium/longevity positioning.
  • Young, airbrushed casting that doesn't match the real 45–70 buyer.
  • Cluttered benefit grids — protect white space and hierarchy.
  • Treating the exosome/stem-cell story as the lead before it's legally cleared.
  • Overusing the activated blue everywhere — it loses its power if it's on every square inch.
Overview: Image Direction

Listings should feel like clinical luxury shot in daylight — bright, airy, high-key lighting with soft natural shadows, never moody or dramatic. Backgrounds stay clean: crisp whites and the pale neutrals from the palette (f7f8f9, d6dee5), with the activated-serum blue reserved as the one hero accent so it always reads as "proof."

Product shots lean into macro texture — a blue serum drop rolling down the frosted bottle, a cream smear catching the light — sharp focus, cool-toned, premium.

Lifestyle imagery features real, mature skin (women and at least one man in the 45–65 range, matching the actual buyer), lightly retouched so pores and natural texture stay visible — this is the "evidence over marketing" audience, so airbrushed 28-year-olds would break trust.

Frame them mid-ritual: serum pressed into a cheekbone, eyes closed in natural window light, the same calm, self-assured tone as the brand's founder-led "answered by Debbi" content.

Overall it should read confident, warm, and understated — closer to a longevity clinic than a beauty-counter promo.

Advertising
Strategic Focus

Advertising runs on two tracks: Branded captures the demand Vitali already creates through email, affiliates, influencers and Google, shoppers who search the brand on Amazon should find Vitali, not a competitor.

Non-branded builds new customer acquisition against the substantial search volume around copper peptides and GHK-Cu, led by the 2% GHK-Cu Serum as the entry point.

Branded protects what exists. Non branded grows it. Non branded spend scales behind the listing, A+ and Store work, traffic is only worth buying once there's a strong experience to receive it. Advertising activates only where inventory can sustain it.

Phased Approach
1
Secure & Establish (0–30 days)

Capture branded search in full and hold visibility across our own product pages. Restructure the existing account and establish a clear performance baseline. Begin non branded activity on the highest intent copper peptide and GHK-Cu searches, alongside discovery to surface terms we haven't anticipated.

2
Expand (30–60 days)

Broaden into benefit-led search, crepey skin, firmness, fine lines, where Vitali's proof points are strongest. Move proven discovery terms into focused campaigns and remove waste. Begin driving shoppers into the rebuilt Brand Store to introduce the wider range.

3
Scale & Reactivate (60–90 days)

Support each returning product with dedicated activity as inventory allows, treating each reactivation as a focused launch. Extend into broader category search now the listing can convert it, begin competitive targeting, and introduce retargeting for shoppers who viewed but didn't buy.

Measuring Success

Branded and non-branded are reported separately. Branded will show strong efficiency immediately, that reflects existing brand strength, not new growth. Non branded progress will show as ranking builds. Some ranking investment runs at deliberately reduced efficiency to build organic visibility and reduce long term reliance on advertising.
